# Back to the Keeper: narration script

Read each numbered line as its own take, with a short pause between lines. Say the line number first if that helps; it gets cut out.

## Episode 1: When do we go back?

### Title

1. Back to the Keeper. Episode one: when do we go back? A pass back to your keeper can be the smartest pass on the pitch, if you pick the right moment.

### Animated steps

2. Step one. When do we go back? Check three things. Are you facing our own goal? Is an attacker close behind you? Is there nothing safe going forward? Then go back to your keeper.
3. Step two. If the attacker is far away and you have time, turn and play forward. Going back every time makes us easy to press. Check over your shoulder before the ball arrives, so you already know which one it is.
4. Step three. Going back is not giving up. It keeps the ball, and your keeper becomes an extra player. The attacker has to chase all the way back, and we can start again on the other side.
5. Keepers, when you see a team-mate facing our goal with an attacker behind, that's your cue. Get off your line, get open to the side, and call yes, nice and loud.

### Napoli v Man City, keeper Ederson

6. First, how it's done. Napoli against Manchester City. City's defender, number 30, has the ball near his own box, facing his own goal, and a Napoli forward is chasing him.
7. He has two choices. One: turn and dribble up the line. Two: pass it back to his keeper, Ederson, out by the side of the goal.
8. Which would you pick?  *(then a countdown: "Which would you pick?")*
9. Back to the keeper. If he turns, the forward is right there. Ederson is off his line and out to the side, ready for it. That's a safe pass, away from the forward.
10. Stop. Now the forward is chasing Ederson. His choices: one, a short pass to his team-mate on the left. Two, kick it long, up the pitch.
11. Keeper, which would you pick?  *(then a countdown: "Keeper: which would you pick?")*
12. Both are safe. His team-mate on the left is open, with no one near him, so the short pass keeps the ball. Ederson doesn't wait for the forward to arrive. One touch, and pass it to the open player.
13. The forward slides in and misses. City keep the ball. A firm pass back to the side, a keeper who's ready out wide, and a quick pass to the open player. That's how it's done.

### Premier League, keeper Jason Steele

14. Now one where it went wrong, so we can learn from it. A Premier League game. A long ball goes over the defence. Watch the defender, in light green, chasing it back toward his own goal.
15. Stop. The defender gets there first, but he's facing his own goal. He has three choices. One: turn up the line. Two: pass inside to his midfielder. Three: pass it back to the keeper.
16. Which would you pick?  *(then a countdown: "Which would you pick?")*
17. Back to the keeper. Turning, the striker is right on his shoulder. Inside, his midfielder is running toward our goal too. The keeper can see the whole pitch. Watch what he does.
18. Good choice. Now you're the keeper. This is his view, and the striker is closing him down. Your choices: one, the midfielder in the middle. Two, the midfielder on the right. Three, the defender who just passed it, out wide, off the picture to the right. Four, kick it long.
19. Keeper, which would you pick?  *(then a countdown: "Keeper: which would you pick?")*
20. One, three and four are all good. The midfielder in the middle is open. The defender out wide is free. Kicking it long is safe. Two is the risky one: the midfielder on the right has an opponent right next to him. Watch what he did.
21. He played it in between them, toward the covered player. It was cut out, and they scored. Keepers: pick the open player, or kick it long. Defenders: after you pass back, get open again.

### Recap

22. Remember the three checks. Facing our goal? Attacker close? Nothing safe forward? Then go back. If you have space and time, turn and play forward. Turn, or return.

## Episode 2: The back-pass rule

### Title

24. Back to the Keeper. Episode two: the back-pass rule. When can the keeper use their hands, and when is it feet only?

### Animated steps

25. Your team-mate kicks the ball back to you on purpose. You're the keeper, inside your own box.
26. Can the keeper pick it up? Say hands, or feet.
27. Feet only. If the keeper picks it up, the other team gets an indirect free kick from the spot where the keeper touched it. If that's inside the small box, the kick moves out to the edge of the small box. It's so close to goal that our whole team may have to stand on the goal line, between the posts. Don't pick it up.
28. Same with a throw-in. If your team-mate throws it straight to you, it's feet only.
29. Your team-mate heads it back to you. Hands, or feet?
30. Hands are fine. The rule is only for kicks and throw-ins. If it comes off a team-mate's head, chest or knee, you can pick it up.
31. No tricks, though. Flicking the ball up with your foot just to head it back is not allowed. The other team gets an indirect free kick where it happened, and the player who started the trick gets a yellow card.
32. Your team-mate tries to kick it clear, but slices it toward our goal. Hands, or feet?
33. Hands are fine. A miskick or a deflection isn't a pass to the keeper, so the keeper can pick it up.
34. So keepers, most back-passes are feet only. That's why every keeper on our team practises receiving with both feet.

### West Ham v Wrexham, 2026

35. Now real games. West Ham against Wrexham. A long ball drops, and a West Ham defender, in claret, is under pressure. Watch how he plays it back.
36. He headed it back to his keeper. Hands, or feet?  *(then a countdown: "Headed back. Hands or feet?")*
37. Hands. It came off his head, not his foot, so the keeper can pick it up. Watch.
38. He picks it up, and the referee lets play go on. That's allowed.

### Sevilla v Inter, Europa League final 2020

39. Same rule with the chest. Sevilla's captain, Jesus Navas, in white, near his own goal.
40. He chests it back. The ball bounces, Bono controls it at his feet, then picks it up with his hands. No free kick. Chest, head or knee: the keeper can use his hands.

### Ipswich v Wolves, 2025

41. Now the other way. Ipswich against Wolves. A defender kicks a simple pass back to his keeper. Watch the keeper's feet.
42. The ball rolls under his foot. Can he grab it with his hands?  *(then a countdown: "Can he grab it?")*
43. No. It was kicked back to him on purpose, so it's feet only, even when you miss it. Watch what the referee gives.
44. An indirect free kick, right next to the goal, and the whole Ipswich team on the goal line. That's what one mistake costs. Feet only.

### Montreal v Toronto, MLS 2016

45. Last one. Montreal against Toronto. Watch the defender in blue.
46. He flicked it up with his foot, headed it back, and the keeper caught it. Is that allowed?  *(then a countdown: "Is that allowed?")*
47. No. That's a trick to get around the rule. The referee missed it. It should have been an indirect free kick and a yellow card for the defender.

### Recap

48. Remember. Kicked back on purpose, or a throw-in: feet only. Head, chest or knee, or a miskick: hands are fine. And no tricks.

## Episode 3: How to pass it back

### Title

50. Back to the Keeper. Episode three: how to pass it back. A good back-pass keeps the ball for your team. A bad one can give away a goal.

### Animated steps

51. Step one. Look first. Keepers move. Before you pass back, check where your keeper is right now.
52. Step two. Talk. You can shout keeper, and point. But your keeper has the best view, so the keeper's call wins. If your keeper shouts, yes, left side, that's where it goes. Away means kick it clear.
53. Step three. Side, not middle. Pass to the side of the goal, never at the middle. If it's missed, a pass to the side rolls wide. A pass to the middle rolls into your own net.
54. Step four. Firm, to the foot. A slow pass lets the attacker get there first. Pass it firm, to your keeper's stronger foot.
55. Keepers, this part is for you. Don't stay on your goal line. Step up to the edge of your box, or out to the side of the goal. Then your defenders know where to look, and you have time on the ball.

### Man City keeper Stefan Ortega

56. Watch Manchester City's keeper, in green. See where he stands before the pass.
57. He has moved out wide, to the side of his goal. His defender has an easy, safe pass.
58. Here he moves into position again, out wide at the side of his goal. His defender has a safe pass back. Watch.
59. Stop. A Newcastle attacker is running at him. His choices: one, the defender who passed it back, now out wide. Two, the midfielder in the middle. Three, kick it long.
60. Keeper, which would you pick?  *(then a countdown: "Keeper: which would you pick?")*
61. One. The defender who passed it back has moved wide, and he's free. The midfielder has a Newcastle player close to him. Kicking long would be safe, but City would probably lose the ball. Ortega stays calm, lets the attacker come to him, then plays it to the free defender.
62. City keep the ball and carry it up the pitch. The keeper was ready out wide, the defender got open again, and the keeper found him. That's a back-pass done right, from start to finish.

### Leicester v Arsenal, keeper Bernd Leno

63. Keepers see the whole pitch, so they talk all game. This is Arsenal's keeper, Bernd Leno, with a microphone on. Turn the sound up and listen to what he shouts.
64. Short, loud and early. A name, then an instruction. Man on. Higher. His team-mates do what he says, because he can see what they can't.
65. And when the keeper wants the ball, the call is: keeper! It's the keeper's call, and it wins.

### Chelsea v Arsenal

66. Chelsea against Arsenal. Watch the pass back to the keeper.
67. Stop. Jorginho, in blue, is about to pass back. Two places he could aim: one, the middle of the goal, where keepers usually stand. Two, out to the side of the net.
68. Which should it be? Find the keeper first.  *(then a countdown: "Which should it be? Find the keeper.")*
69. Two. Kepa, in green, is out to the side of his net. That's where this pass should go. Watch where it goes.
70. The pass went to the middle of the goal, where keepers usually stand. But Kepa had moved to the side. Look first. Pass to where your keeper is.

### Japan v USA, U-20 Women's World Cup

71. Japan against the USA, at the Under-20 Women's World Cup. Watch the speed of this back-pass.
72. Stop. Who gets to the ball first, the keeper or the attacker?  *(then a countdown: "Who gets to the ball first?")*
73. The attacker. The pass is too slow.
74. Now, keepers, watch Iwasaki. She starts deep, near her goal, and she is backing up.
75. Then she charges out. Coming out makes the goal smaller for the attacker. There is much less net to shoot at.
76. She blocks it, but it was close. Defenders, pass it firm. Keepers, be ready early, at the edge of your box.

### Recap

77. Remember. Look first. The keeper calls it. Side, not middle. Firm, to the foot. And side, not middle works all over the pitch. When we build out from the back, or clear a ball from the middle, we aim for the side.

78. Thanks for watching. See you at training.
